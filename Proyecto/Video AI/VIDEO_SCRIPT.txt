==============================================================================
 3D BOIDS FLOCKING DASHBOARD — VIDEO SCRIPT (target runtime ~5:00)
==============================================================================

Format:
  Action: what the presenter does on screen (the screen recording).
  Voice:  the voice-over, spoken verbatim while that action happens.

Pacing note: aim for a calm, steady delivery (~140 words/min). Pause briefly
between sections. Total spoken text is sized for roughly five minutes.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[00:00 — 00:30]  SECTION 1 — INTRO / TITLE
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Action: The application window is already open, paused, showing the dark
        dashboard. The 3D cube with the static flock fills the left side; the
        controls, metrics and charts sit in the sidebar on the right. Slowly
        drag the mouse once across the 3D view so the camera rotates gently
        around the cube.

Voice:  "Hi, and welcome. In this video I'll walk you through a real-time
        three-dimensional flocking simulation — a Boids model — built entirely
        in Python. The whole thing runs locally on a regular laptop, using
        PyQt6 for the interface, pyqtgraph and OpenGL for the 3D rendering,
        and NumPy for the math. What you're looking at is the dashboard:
        a large 3D viewport on the left, and a control panel, live metrics,
        and real-time charts on the right."

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[00:30 — 01:05]  SECTION 2 — THE 3D SCENE AND CAMERA
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Action: Slowly drag to rotate the camera all the way around the cube. Then
        scroll to zoom in toward the center, and scroll back out. Briefly
        point the cursor at the ground grid, then at the colored XYZ axis
        arrows in the corner.

Voice:  "The simulation lives inside a bounded three-dimensional cube. You can
        see the wire-frame box that marks its limits, a ground grid for
        reference, and the colored X-Y-Z axis arrows in the corner so we
        always know our orientation. The camera is fully interactive — I can
        drag to orbit around the flock, scroll to zoom in and out, and pan to
        reframe the scene. Each boid is drawn as a small oriented dart that
        points in its direction of travel, and they're colored by speed, from
        deep blue for the slower ones up to bright cyan for the fastest."

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[01:05 — 01:35]  SECTION 3 — STARTING THE SIMULATION
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Action: Move the cursor to the sidebar and click the "Start" button. The flock
        begins moving. Let it run for a few seconds so the boids visibly
        organize into moving groups. Rotate the camera slightly to follow them.

Voice:  "Let's bring it to life. I'll click Start. Now the two hundred and
        fifty boids begin to move. Notice that nobody is steering them
        globally — each boid only follows three simple local rules, and the
        flocking behavior you see emerges from those rules alone. Watch how
        loose individuals quickly gather into coherent, swirling groups that
        flow around the cube. And because the boundaries wrap around, a boid
        that leaves one face of the cube reappears on the opposite side, with
        its neighbors still able to see it across the edge."

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[01:35 — 02:35]  SECTION 4 — THE THREE FLOCKING RULES (SLIDERS)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Action: With the simulation running, slowly drag the "Cohesion Strength"
        slider up to near its maximum. Pause so the viewer sees the flock
        tighten into denser clumps. Then drag it back down toward zero and let
        the flock spread out and scatter.

Voice:  "The behavior is controlled by three rules, and each one has a live
        slider — changes take effect instantly. First, cohesion: the urge to
        move toward the average position of nearby flockmates. As I raise it,
        the boids pull together into tight, dense clusters. And as I lower it
        back down, that gravitational pull weakens and the flock spreads out
        and drifts apart."

Action: Now slowly raise the "Separation Strength" slider to a high value.
        The dense clumps loosen and the boids keep a clear personal distance.

Voice:  "Second, separation: the rule that keeps boids from crowding each
        other. As I increase it, you can see them push apart and maintain a
        comfortable spacing, so even when they travel together they no longer
        overlap."

Action: Return separation to a moderate value. Then slowly raise the
        "Alignment Strength" slider. The groups start moving in cleaner,
        more parallel streams.

Voice:  "And third, alignment: the tendency to match the heading of nearby
        neighbors. As I turn it up, the motion becomes far more orderly —
        the boids fall into the same direction and you get those smooth,
        sweeping streams flying in formation. Together, just these three
        rules — separation, alignment, and cohesion — produce surprisingly
        lifelike flocking."

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[02:35 — 03:15]  SECTION 5 — SPEED AND PERCEPTION
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Action: Drag the "Boid Speed" slider up. The flock visibly accelerates. Drag
        it back down to a calm cruising speed.

Voice:  "There are two more controls. Boid speed sets how fast they travel.
        Turning it up sends the whole flock racing around the cube, and easing
        it back down returns them to a calm cruising pace."

Action: Slowly raise the "Perception Radius" slider to a large value, pause,
        then lower it to a small value. The flock merges into one big group at
        high perception, and breaks into many small independent groups at low
        perception.

Voice:  "And perception radius controls how far each boid can 'see' its
        neighbors. With a large radius, every boid is aware of almost everyone
        else, so the whole population tends to merge into one giant
        super-flock. With a small radius, each boid only reacts to its closest
        companions, and the flock fragments into many small, independent
        groups that wander on their own."

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[03:15 — 04:05]  SECTION 6 — LIVE METRICS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Action: Move the cursor to the "Live Metrics" panel and slowly point at each
        value in turn — total boids, average speed, average neighbor distance,
        cluster count, and runtime — as they update.

Voice:  "While all of this is happening, the dashboard measures the flock in
        real time. The metrics panel shows the total number of boids; the
        average speed across the whole flock; the average nearest-neighbor
        distance, which tells us how tightly packed they are; an estimated
        cluster count, computed by finding connected groups of nearby boids;
        and the total simulation runtime."

Action: Slowly drag the "Cohesion Strength" slider up again while keeping the
        metrics panel in view. Point at the "Cluster count" and "Avg neighbor
        distance" values as they change.

Voice:  "And these aren't just decoration — they respond to what we do. Watch
        the cluster count and the average neighbor distance as I increase
        cohesion again. As the boids pull together, the neighbor distance
        drops and the many separate clusters merge into just a handful. The
        numbers track the behavior we can see in the viewport."

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[04:05 — 04:40]  SECTION 7 — REAL-TIME CHARTS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Action: Move the cursor down to the "Live Charts" section. Point at each of the
        three scrolling plots in turn: Average Speed, Average Neighbor
        Distance, and Cluster Count. Then make one more slider change — for
        example lower perception radius — and let the charts visibly react.

Voice:  "Below the metrics, three live charts plot the same quantities over
        time on a rolling window: average speed, average neighbor distance, and
        cluster count. These let us see the dynamics — not just the current
        value, but the trend. So when I change a parameter, like dropping the
        perception radius here, you can watch the cluster-count line climb as
        the flock breaks apart, and the neighbor-distance line shift in
        response. It turns the simulation into a small live experiment."

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[04:40 — 05:00]  SECTION 8 — RESET AND CLOSING
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Action: Click the "Pause" button — the flock freezes. Then click "Reset" — the
        boids scatter back to random positions and the charts clear. Optionally
        click "Start" once more for a final few seconds of motion, then slowly
        orbit the camera as the closing line is spoken.

Voice:  "Finally, the transport controls: I can Pause to freeze the flock at
        any moment, and Reset to scatter the boids back to random positions and
        clear the history. And that's the project — a complete, real-time 3D
        Boids flocking dashboard in Python, where complex, organic group
        behavior emerges from three simple rules, all visualized and measured
        live. Thanks for watching."
