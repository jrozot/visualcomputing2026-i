[00:00 — 01:00]  CHALLENGE 1 — NEIGHBORS ACROSS A WRAP-AROUND WORLD
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Code(boids/simulation.py:94-102):  scroll to and highlight the displacement /
        distance block — the lines building `diff`, the wrap correction
        `diff -= b * np.round(diff / b)`, the squared-distance `einsum`, and
        the `np.fill_diagonal(dist2, np.inf)`.

Problem: The cube uses wrap-around boundaries, so a boid leaving one face
        reappears on the opposite side. A naive distance calculation treats
        two boids on opposite faces as far apart, which broke flocking near
        the edges. The fix wraps each pairwise displacement into the
        half-cube range before measuring distance, so neighbors are detected
        correctly across the boundary.

Voice:  "The first real challenge came from the world being wrap-around — when
        a boid exits one side of the cube, it comes back on the other side. At
        first I just measured the straight-line distance between every pair of
        boids, and the flocking looked broken near the edges. Two boids that
        were actually right next to each other across a boundary were being
        treated as if they were on opposite ends of the world. The fix is this
        wrap correction here: before I compute any distances, I fold each
        displacement vector into the range of half a cube in each direction.
        That way 'across the edge' is correctly seen as 'close.' Then I square
        it to get the distance matrix, and I set the diagonal to infinity so a
        boid never counts itself as its own neighbor. After that, the flock
        behaved consistently everywhere, including right at the borders."

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[01:00 — 02:00]  CHALLENGE 2 — JITTERY, UNSTABLE MOTION
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Code(boids/simulation.py:127-137):  highlight the heading-integration and
        speed-clamp block — `self.vel += acc * p.turn_rate`, the speed
        magnitude, the `min_speed` floor, and the `np.clip(...)` that keeps
        speed inside a band. Optionally also glance at lines 38-39 where
        `turn_rate` and `min_speed_ratio` are defined.

Problem: Applying the combined steering forces directly made boids snap to new
        headings every frame, producing nervous, twitchy motion, and some
        boids would slow almost to a stop or accelerate without limit. The
        current code scales the steering by a turn-rate so direction changes
        are gradual, and clamps the speed into a fixed band so every boid keeps
        moving smoothly.

Voice:  "The second challenge was just making it look good. When I first added
        the three flocking rules and applied the combined steering straight to
        the velocity, the boids twitched — they'd snap to a new direction every
        single frame, so the whole flock looked nervous instead of organic. On
        top of that, some boids would almost stop dead while others sped up
        endlessly. There were really two fixes. The first is this turn-rate
        factor: instead of letting the steering fully redirect a boid each
        frame, I scale it down, so headings change gradually and the motion
        becomes smooth and curved. The second is the speed clamp right below
        it — I take each boid's speed and squeeze it into a fixed band between a
        minimum and a maximum. That guarantees nobody freezes and nobody runs
        away, so the entire flock cruises at a believable, consistent pace."

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[02:00 — 03:00]  CHALLENGE 3 — KEEPING IT SMOOTH IN REAL TIME
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Code(boids/simulation.py:144-146):  highlight where the already-computed
        distance matrix is reused to derive the nearest-neighbor metric and
        feed `_update_metrics`. Then switch files.
Code(boids/main_window.py:28 and 153-157):  highlight `CHART_EVERY = 6` and the
        `if self._frame % CHART_EVERY == 0:` block that throttles chart updates.

Problem: Computing metrics and redrawing the charts on every frame, on top of
        the per-frame physics, was enough extra work to make the animation
        stutter. The current design reuses the distance matrix that the
        simulation already built — instead of recomputing it — and only
        refreshes the charts every few frames, keeping the simulation loop
        light enough to stay smooth.

Voice:  "The last challenge was performance — keeping everything smooth in real
        time. With a few hundred boids, the heavy part each frame is comparing
        every boid against every other boid. The mistake I made early on was
        doing that comparison once for the physics, and then doing more
        expensive passes again just to compute the live metrics. So here, the
        simulation reuses the exact distance information it already calculated
        for the movement to also produce the nearest-neighbor and cluster
        numbers — no duplicated work. The other cost was the charts. Redrawing
        three plots on every single frame was enough to make the animation
        stutter, and honestly you can't see updates that fast anyway. So over
        in the main window, this counter only refreshes the charts every few
        frames instead of every one. Between reusing the heavy computation and
        easing off on the chart redraws, the simulation loop stays light and
        the animation stays fluid. Those three fixes — handling the wrap-around
        world, smoothing the motion, and trimming the per-frame cost — are what
        took it from a rough prototype to something that actually feels polished."
